In April 2020, Silver Lake invested $1 billion in Airbnb.

Travel was illegal. Airbnb had cut 25% of its workforce. Its valuation had collapsed from $31B to $18B. People called it reckless.

Eight months later, Airbnb IPO'd at $68 billion.

Silver Lake's co-CEO used one word that stuck with me: "inevitably." He wasn't predicting timing. He was asserting direction.

I'll be honest — I find it uncomfortable to write about opportunity when people are suffering through real conflicts. But history keeps showing me the same pattern: crisis doesn't destroy opportunity. It relocates it. And capital that flows toward recovery isn't just profit-seeking. It funds what gets rebuilt.

Look at what COVID looked like in real-time vs. what it became:

→ "Travel industry destroyed" became Airbnb's $68B IPO
→ "Remote work chaos" became a permanent 43% hybrid shift
→ "Mental health crisis" became a $5B+ app market
→ "Supply chain collapse" became a $300B+ tech investment wave

Now translate that lens to 2026:

→ European energy dependency → $1T+ green energy investment
→ Defense tech urgency → next civilian AI/autonomy wave
→ Ukraine destruction → $588B reconstruction pipeline
→ Capital fleeing conflict zones → Dubai, Singapore, Switzerland strengthening

The pattern is mechanical, not moral. Every major crisis has been a wealth-transfer event — not to the lucky, but to the prepared and contrarian.

It requires exactly one skill: the ability to distinguish between temporary crisis and permanent change.

Silver Lake didn't have better information than everyone else in April 2020. They had better questions. Not "how bad will this get?" but "where is this inevitably going?"

What's an asset or opportunity you've spotted that looks scary right now but feels structurally inevitable?
