# Twitter/X Thread

1/ The most impressive AI agent demo you've seen is probably irrelevant to where agents are actually creating value.

That viral "book me a restaurant in Paris" demo? Stunning to watch. Almost nothing to do with reality.

Here's what's actually working: 🧵

2/ While everyone debated whether AI agents were overhyped, something quieter happened.

Enterprise finance teams cut invoice processing time by 80%.

Not with flashy demos. With boring, repetitive cognitive work done at scale.

3/ The three places AI agents are genuinely delivering right now:

- Cognitive repetition (invoices, lead qualification, support triage)
- Developer inner loop (absorbing boilerplate so engineers can think)
- Real-time decision support (supply chain, healthcare, finance)

4/ Here's the part nobody talks about:

Most people assume AI agents fail because the AI isn't smart enough.

Wrong.

The leading causes of failure are integration gaps and data quality problems. Not model intelligence.

5/ 63% of executives cite "platform sprawl" as a growing concern with AI agents.

The bottleneck isn't artificial intelligence.

It's organizational integration.

That's a much harder problem to solve -- and a much less exciting one to tweet about.

6/ The productivity-revenue gap is revealing:

66% of orgs report productivity gains from AI agents.
Only 20% report revenue growth.
But 74% hope for it.

Productivity without revenue means the workflow redesign hasn't happened yet.

7/ The companies winning with AI agents aren't the ones that deployed the most.

They're the ones that redesigned workflows around agents -- not bolted agents onto existing workflows.

That distinction sounds subtle. It's actually everything.

8/ Here's my takeaway:

The AI agent era isn't arriving in a single dramatic moment.

It's arriving in the back-office, one workflow at a time.

The most valuable AI work is the work nobody posts demos of.

9/ If you're evaluating AI agents for your team, stop asking "what can this agent do?"

Start asking:
- Where is our data clean enough?
- Which workflows are integration-ready?
- What boring work eats the most hours?

That's where agents actually deliver.

10/ We're watching the wrong demos and expecting the wrong breakthroughs.

The real AI agent revolution looks like an 80% reduction in invoice processing time.

Not glamorous. Just useful.

# Podcast Q&A Script

## Exchange 1

**Host:** "So there's this viral AI agent demo -- books a restaurant, checks the weather, updates your calendar. Everyone lost their minds over it. But you're saying that's basically irrelevant?"

**Guest:** "Not irrelevant exactly, but misleading. It shows what agents could become, not what they are. The real value right now is deeply unsexy -- invoice processing, support triage, developer boilerplate. One enterprise finance team cut invoice time by 80%. Nobody's making viral videos about that, but that's where the actual ROI lives. We're watching the highlight reel and ignoring the box score."

## Exchange 2

**Host:** "You mention that most agent failures aren't actually about the AI being bad. That surprised me. What's really going wrong?"

**Guest:** "This is the part that took me a while to accept. We want the problem to be 'the model needs to be smarter' because that's a solvable, exciting problem. But 63% of executives point to platform sprawl. The real failures are integration gaps and messy data. Your agent can be brilliant, but if it's pulling from three disconnected systems with inconsistent data formats, it's going to produce garbage. The bottleneck is organizational, not technological. And that's uncomfortable because it means the fix is change management, not better AI."

## Exchange 3

**Host:** "66% productivity gains but only 20% revenue growth -- what do you make of that gap?"

**Guest:** "That gap is the whole story, honestly. Productivity gains mean agents are doing tasks faster. Revenue growth means the business model shifted around them. Most companies bolted agents onto existing workflows and said 'look, we're faster.' But faster at the same thing isn't transformation. The 20% seeing revenue growth? They redesigned the workflow entirely. They asked different questions. Not 'how do we speed this up' but 'what becomes possible now that this step takes minutes instead of days.' That reframing is where the real value unlocks."
