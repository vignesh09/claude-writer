LINKEDIN POST:

I spent two years waiting for AI to get smarter.

It kept getting smarter. It never got more useful.

GPT-4 arrived. Claude 3 arrived. Each one measurably better at reasoning, writing, summarizing. And I still ended every AI interaction the same way: copy the output, fix what it got wrong, re-explain context it had already forgotten, and finish the work myself.

I assumed the problem was capability.
It was actually architecture.

What was missing wasn't intelligence. It was task-specific intelligence -- the ability to understand not just a subject, but how my actual work flows. What tools to reach for. What sequence to follow. What "done" looks like.

Here's the uncomfortable truth about most AI usage today: it's a glorified demo.

Impressive in a controlled setting. 70% right on your real work. You still manage every handoff, hold all the context, and do the last mile yourself.

The shift that changed things for me wasn't a smarter model. It was encoding workflow knowledge directly into AI -- what Anthropic calls Skills.

The difference:
-> Conversational AI: you ask, it answers, you do the rest
-> Skills-based AI: you delegate the workflow, not just the question

My test for whether an AI tool is genuinely useful: can I hand it a task and walk away?

For most AI tools, the answer is still no.
With skills, the answer is increasingly yes.

It turns out "smarter" and "useful" are not the same thing. Specialized beats general-purpose almost every time.

What's been your experience -- are you still finishing AI's work, or has something actually changed how you use it?
