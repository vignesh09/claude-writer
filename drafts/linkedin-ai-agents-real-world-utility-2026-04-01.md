The most impressive AI agent demo you've seen is probably the least useful one.

An agent that books a Paris restaurant from a single prompt? Genuinely stunning. Also genuinely irrelevant to where AI agents are actually delivering value right now.

While we debated whether agentic AI was overhyped, something quieter happened. Finance teams cut invoice processing time by 80%. Support queues started triaging themselves. Coding agents absorbed the boilerplate so developers could actually think.

The boring stuff. At scale.

Here's what surprised me most: when AI agents fail in the enterprise, it's almost never because the AI isn't smart enough. The leading causes are integration gaps and data quality problems. 63% of executives cite platform sprawl as a growing concern.

The bottleneck isn't intelligence. It's organizational plumbing.

And the numbers tell a split story:

→ 66% of organizations report productivity gains
→ Only 20% report actual revenue growth
→ 74% are still hoping for it

That gap reveals everything. The companies winning aren't deploying the most agents. They're redesigning workflows around them.

The AI agent era isn't arriving in a single dramatic moment. It's arriving in the back-office, one workflow at a time.

Where are you seeing AI agents actually work--not in demos, but in daily operations?
